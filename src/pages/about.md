Biomuse publishes practical guides for people moving between countries — and for the financial life that comes with it.

Most of what is written about relocation falls into two camps. Government pages are accurate but written for officials. Everything else is marketing dressed as advice, produced by firms who want to sell you a service. Someone weighing up a residency route, or working out whether they can get a mortgage without a local credit file, deserves a clear explanation with the real requirements and costs in it.

## What we cover

- **Visas and residency** — the routes worth understanding, what each requires, and who qualifies
- **Housing** — renting and buying in a new country, including without a local credit history
- **Banking and money** — accounts, transfers, private banking, and building a credit file from nothing
- **Mortgages** — cross-border borrowing, first-time buyers, and thin-file lending
- **Tax** — the reporting obligations that follow you across borders
- **Work and relocation** — roles that sponsor, what they pay, and the practical first months

## How we produce our content

- **We start from real questions** — what people actually search for when facing these decisions.
- **We work from primary sources** — government guidance, published fee schedules, lender and provider terms.
- **We use technology in our workflow, with human oversight.** Research and drafting are assisted by modern tools, and articles are reviewed before publication.
- **We update rather than abandon.** Fees, thresholds and eligibility rules change. Guides carry a last-reviewed date and are revised on a rolling schedule.
- **We show the arithmetic.** Where a decision involves cost, the numbers are laid out so you can check them against your own situation.

## What we are not

We are not a law firm, an immigration adviser, a tax practice, a lender, a broker, or a regulated financial adviser. We do not submit applications, arrange credit, or represent anyone.

Rules, fees and thresholds change frequently and apply differently depending on your nationality, circumstances and timing. Nothing here is legal, immigration, tax or financial advice. Before you act, verify the current position with the relevant official source and take advice from a qualified professional in the country concerned.
