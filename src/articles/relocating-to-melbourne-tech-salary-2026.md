**Melbourne's tech sector is paying internationally competitive salaries in 2026, and for skilled workers arriving from overseas, the city offers a genuinely high quality of life — but the real cost of living, tax system, and rental market contain enough complexity to derail a relocation that looks straightforward on paper.** **This guide walks you through what an AUD 130,000 tech salary actually gets you, where the hidden costs sit, and what financial infrastructure to build before you land.**

---

## Why This Matters in 2026

Melbourne's technology industry has undergone a structural shift in the last two years. The concentration of cloud infrastructure, fintech, health-tech, and AI operations in the inner suburbs — particularly around the Melbourne CBD, Cremorne ("Silicon Yarra"), and Docklands — has tightened the labour market for software engineers, data scientists, DevOps specialists, and product managers. Employers are actively recruiting internationally, and sponsorship for skilled tech workers has become a routine part of hiring strategy for mid-to-large employers.

At the same time, the cost of living has accelerated. Melbourne rents rose sharply in 2023–2024 and have stabilised at a high plateau. Grocery inflation, utility costs following energy market reforms, and the ongoing housing supply shortage mean that a salary that looked generous two years ago buys less than it once did. For overseas recruits arriving with no Australian credit history, no local rental references, and unfamiliar with ATO (Australian Taxation Office) obligations, the financial first year can be genuinely stressful without preparation.

The 2026 context matters for one additional reason: Australia's immigration policy is actively prioritising skilled tech workers through reformed visa streams, but those visa conditions directly affect your tax treatment, your ability to borrow for property, and your access to certain financial products. The interaction between your immigration status and your personal finances is not incidental — it is central to planning your relocation well.

### Superannuation: The Benefit Newcomers Undervalue

Superannuation is not deducted from your take-home pay — it is a mandatory employer contribution on top of your salary, currently set at 11.5% of your ordinary time earnings. On AUD 130,000, that is approximately AUD 14,950 per year going into a super fund in your name. For tech workers relocating for two to five years, this is genuine wealth accumulation, and departing temporary residents can access their super when they leave Australia permanently through a Departing Australia Superannuation Payment (DASP) — though this is taxed at a flat rate. A financial adviser can help you assess the trade-offs.

## Banking and Money: Building Your Financial Infrastructure

Arriving in Melbourne without Australian financial infrastructure is the single most common cause of a stressful first month. You cannot sign a lease without proof of income and identification. Your employer needs your bank account details from day one. And if you are sending money from overseas to cover your bond and first month's rent, international transfer costs add up fast.

### Before You Arrive

1. **Open an Australian bank account remotely.** Several major banks allow applications up to three months before arrival. You will activate the account in-branch. Research which banks support this and bring your passport and visa documentation.

2. **Set up a multi-currency account for the transition period.** During the weeks between leaving your home country and getting your first Australian payslip, you will need to access funds in AUD. A [multi-currency account designed for expats](/multi-currency-accounts-compared-expats/) can hold AUD, USD, GBP, EUR, and other currencies simultaneously, letting you convert strategically rather than at airport kiosks or bank counters.

3. **Plan your international money transfers.** Moving your savings or security deposit from overseas to Australia via a traditional bank wire is expensive. Specialist transfer services routinely save 1–3% on the exchange rate, which matters enormously when you are moving AUD 20,000–50,000. Read our full breakdown of the [cheapest ways to move money between countries](/cheapest-ways-to-move-money-between-countries/) before you initiate any large transfer.

### Building Australian Credit History

Australia's credit reporting system works differently from the US or UK model, but the underlying challenge is the same: you arrive with no local history. Banks, landlords, and eventually lenders all want to see evidence that you manage financial obligations. The good news is that Australia operates a "comprehensive credit reporting" (CCR) system that records positive data — on-time payments — not just defaults. Pay every bill on time from month one and your file builds quickly.

The broader challenge of [building a credit file from nothing in a new country](/how-to-build-credit-file-from-nothing-new-country/) is something our full guide covers in detail, including secured credit cards, credit-builder accounts, and the timeline you should realistically expect before applying for significant credit.

## Renting in Melbourne as a New Arrival

Melbourne's rental market in 2026 is competitive. Vacancy rates in inner suburbs remain low, and applications are frequently competitive with multiple candidates for desirable properties. As an overseas arrival, you face two specific disadvantages: no Australian rental history and no local employer payslips in the first weeks.

### What Landlords and Property Managers Typically Want

- Evidence of income: employment contract or first payslip, and/or bank statements showing savings
- Identification: passport and visa
- Rental references: from overseas landlords with contact details, translated if necessary
- Bond: typically four weeks' rent in Victoria (there are legal caps)
- Some property managers accept a character reference from an employer

### Practical Tactics for Competitive Applications

- Apply with a cover letter explaining your situation — property managers respond well to transparency
- Offer to pay two to three months' rent in advance if your savings allow (check Victorian tenancy law on what landlords can legally accept in advance)
- Use a reputable relocation agent who has established relationships with property management companies
- Avoid starting your lease search from overseas — attend inspections in person where possible, or have a trusted local contact attend on your behalf

For your first weeks, a furnished short-term rental or serviced apartment is almost always the right call. It gives you a local address for bank account activation, a period of stability while you attend property inspections, and breathing room without a 12-month lease commitment.

## Tax: What You Must Understand Before Day One

Australian tax is a topic where the stakes of getting it wrong are high and the interaction with your home-country obligations can be complicated.

### Australian Tax Residency

Australia's tax residency rules are applied under a facts-and-circumstances test. In most cases, a person who intends to live and work in Australia on a sponsored employer visa will be treated as a tax resident from their first day in Australia. As a resident, you are taxed on your worldwide income — including any interest, dividends, or rental income from assets in your home country.

Whether you also owe tax on the same income in your home country depends on whether a double tax agreement (DTA) exists between Australia and your home country, and what its provisions say. Australia has agreements with over 40 countries including the United States, United Kingdom, India, Germany, Japan, Canada, and China. The mechanics of how these treaties actually prevent double taxation — and where they still leave you with a liability — are more nuanced than most newcomers realise.

**This is not an area to navigate alone.** Engage a CPA with cross-border tax experience before you become Australian tax resident, not six months after you file your first return. Your home-country obligations do not automatically disappear because you have moved.

### Your Home-Country Obligations: A Note for US Citizens and Green Card Holders

If you are a US citizen or Green Card holder relocating to Melbourne, the United States continues to tax you on your worldwide income regardless of where you live. The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE) and Foreign Tax Credit mechanisms exist to mitigate double taxation, but the interaction with Australian tax is genuinely complex — particularly for superannuation, which the US does not automatically recognise as a pension for treaty purposes. A US-qualified CPA or tax attorney with expat experience is not optional in this situation.

## Property: The Long-Game Approach

Most tech workers who relocate to Melbourne do not buy property in their first year. The reasons are practical:

1. **FIRB approval**: Foreign nationals typically require Foreign Investment Review Board approval to purchase residential property in Australia. Approval fees apply and are scaled to purchase price.
2. **Additional stamp duty**: Victoria charges a surcharge on foreign purchasers of residential property (the rate has varied; check current rates with a conveyancer).
3. **Deposit requirements**: Lenders generally require 20% deposits from borrowers without established Australian financial history, and some lenders restrict lending to temporary visa holders.
4. **Credit history**: Building enough local financial history to qualify for a competitive mortgage rate takes at least 12–24 months of consistent Australian credit activity.

If you are planning ahead for eventual property purchase — whether in Australia or back home — understanding deposit requirements and mortgage structures is worthwhile early. Our guides to [mortgage deposit requirements by loan type](/deposit-requirements-by-loan-type/) and [getting a mortgage with a thin credit file](/getting-mortgage-thin-credit-file/) are written for exactly this situation, even if the examples are US-market focused, as the underlying principles around deposit sizing, LTV ratios, and lender risk appetite translate broadly.

## 7 Common Mistakes — and How to Avoid Them

1. **Signing a 12-month lease from overseas without seeing the property.**
   *Solution:* Commit only to a short-term furnished rental for the first four to eight weeks. Use that time to attend inspections in person and make an informed long-term decision.

2. **Using your home-country bank for all AUD spending in the first months.**
   *Solution:* Open an Australian bank account before you arrive. The foreign transaction fees and exchange-rate margins on daily spending from an overseas card add up to hundreds of dollars per month.

3. **Assuming your home-country tax obligations end when you board the plane.**
   *Solution:* Speak to a CPA before you depart, not after. If you are a US person, your filing obligations continue regardless of residency. If a tax treaty applies, it needs to be applied correctly.

4. **Sending a large sum via bank wire to fund your bond and moving costs.**
   *Solution:* Use a specialist international money transfer service. On a AUD 30,000 transfer, the difference between a bank wire and a specialist service can easily exceed AUD 600–900.

5. **Ignoring superannuation because it feels distant and complicated.**
   *Solution:* Choose your super fund promptly when you start work (Australia's "stapled fund" rules mean you may be defaulted to a previous employer's fund, or to a MySuper default). Fees vary significantly between funds.

6. **Underestimating the upfront cash requirements of renting.**
   *Solution:* Budget for four weeks' bond plus two weeks' rent in advance (legally mandated maximums in Victoria) plus moving costs, furniture for an unfurnished apartment, and connection fees. On a AUD 3,000/month apartment, that is AUD 12,000–15,000 before you buy a single piece of furniture.

7. **Accepting the first salary offer without benchmarking it to local market rates.**
   *Solution:* Use multiple salary surveys (SEEK, LinkedIn Salary Insights, industry reports) to understand what Melbourne employers are actually paying for your specific role and experience level before you negotiate. Tech salaries in Australia are not simply a USD-to-AUD conversion of US rates.

## Worked Illustrative Example: Priya's First Year

*The following is a fictional illustrative example. All figures are used for educational purposes only.*

Priya is a senior data engineer with eight years of experience who relocates from Bangalore to Melbourne on an employer-sponsored TSS visa in March 2026. Her offer is AUD 145,000 gross, plus 11.5% superannuation.

**Before she arrives**, Priya opens an Australian bank account remotely, sets up a multi-currency account to hold her savings in INR and AUD, and uses a specialist transfer service to move AUD 25,000 to her new Australian account for bond and setup costs. The rate she gets saves her approximately AUD 700 compared to her Indian bank's wire service.

**On arrival**, she books a furnished apartment in Southbank for eight weeks at AUD 3,800/month (all inclusive) while she attends rental inspections. In week three, she secures a 12-month lease on an unfurnished two-bedroom in Brunswick at AUD 2,550/month — materially below the inner-suburb average — and pays four weeks' bond (AUD 5,100) plus two weeks' advance rent.

**Tax**: Priya engages a Melbourne-based CPA with India-Australia cross-border experience before filing anything. The Australia-India DTA applies, and her CPA helps her understand how her Indian bank interest income is treated. She lodges her first Australian tax return for the 2025–26 financial year correctly, on time.

**Super**: Priya's employer contributes AUD 16,675 into superannuation during her first full financial year. She selects an industry super fund after comparing fees and long-term performance. She does not touch this money during her stay, and when she eventually returns to India permanently after four years, she assesses the DASP option with her CPA.

**By month 18**, Priya has accumulated approximately AUD 45,000 in savings and is beginning to research the Melbourne property market — mindful that FIRB approval and the foreign purchaser duty surcharge will affect her buying costs if she proceeds while still on a temporary visa.

## Cross-Border Finances: Keeping Your Home-Country Accounts Active

Many tech workers relocating to Melbourne maintain significant assets in their home country — bank accounts, investment accounts, property, or retirement savings. This is entirely normal, but it creates obligations.

Australian tax residents must declare worldwide income. If you have overseas bank accounts or investments generating income, that income is assessable in Australia. Depending on where you are from, your home country may also continue to have claims on that income, which is where treaty provisions become critical.

If you have been holding US-domiciled investment accounts, the questions around non-resident account access and reporting add another layer of complexity. Our article on [best brokerage accounts for non-resident aliens](/best-brokerage-accounts-non-resident-aliens/) covers how US brokerage accounts interact with non-US residency, which is directly relevant to US citizens moving to Melbourne who want to maintain their existing US investment accounts.

For workers who are not US persons but have investment portfolios in their home country, the key question is whether your home-country broker will continue to service your account once you become an Australian resident. Some jurisdictions require brokers to restrict services to non-residents. Check with your provider before you depart.

## Health Insurance: The Medicare and Private Cover Question

Australia's public healthcare system, Medicare, is available to citizens, permanent residents, and — crucially — temporary visa holders from countries that have reciprocal healthcare agreements with Australia. These include the UK, Ireland, New Zealand, Belgium, Finland, Italy, Malta, the Netherlands, Norway, Slovenia, and Sweden. If your home country is on that list, you have immediate access to Medicare from the day you register.

If your home country is not on that list (India, the US, China, and most others are not), you will need private health insurance as a condition of many visa types, and it is strongly advisable regardless. Basic hospital cover from a major fund costs approximately AUD 130–180/month for a single person in 2026. Factor this into your budget modelling from day one.

Private health insurance also carries a tax dimension: the Medicare Levy Surcharge applies to Australian residents (including most temporary residents on applicable visas) who earn above a threshold and do not hold private hospital cover. A CPA can confirm whether you are liable.

## The Bigger Picture: Melbourne as a Long-Term Base

For tech workers considering Melbourne not just as a temporary posting but as a longer-term base or pathway to permanent residency, the financial calculus shifts. Employer-sponsored visa pathways can lead to permanent residency nomination, and permanent residents access a substantially broader range of financial products — including more mortgage options, access to First Home Owner Grant schemes (subject to eligibility), and removal of FIRB residential property restrictions after certain residency milestones.

The decision about whether to pursue permanent residency, when to apply, and how your financial life should be structured in the meantime is one where immigration and financial planning intersect tightly.
