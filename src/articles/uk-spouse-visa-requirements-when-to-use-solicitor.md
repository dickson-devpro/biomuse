**The UK spouse visa — formally a leave to enter or remain under Appendix FM of the Immigration Rules — requires a UK-based sponsor to meet a £29,000 gross income threshold in 2026, alongside substantial documentary evidence of a genuine relationship and adequate accommodation. Most refusals are preventable: they stem from documentation gaps, misunderstood financial rules, or missed eligibility details that a careful review would have caught before submission.**

---

## Why This Matters in 2026

The UK spouse visa landscape shifted materially in 2024 and early 2025. The government raised the minimum income requirement in stages — from the historic £18,600 set in 2012 all the way to £29,000 — as part of a broader package of immigration controls. For foreign nationals sponsoring a spouse from abroad, particularly those new to UK employment or working in sectors with lower average wages, meeting this threshold is now a genuine planning challenge rather than an administrative formality.

Simultaneously, caseworker scrutiny of relationship evidence has intensified. UKVI guidance emphasises assessing whether a relationship is "genuine and subsisting," and refusal letters frequently cite insufficient evidence of cohabitation, contact, and shared life. Understanding what caseworkers actually look for — and preparing evidence to match — is the practical work at the heart of a successful application.

This article walks through every core requirement, explains the financial rules in plain language with illustrative worked examples, highlights the most common application mistakes and their solutions, and gives you an honest framework for deciding whether a solicitor is worth the cost for your specific circumstances.

## Who Can Apply: Basic Eligibility at a Glance

The spouse or partner visa (Entry Clearance) is available when:

- The applicant is outside the UK and wishes to join a UK-based sponsor.
- The sponsor is a British citizen, someone with settled status (Indefinite Leave to Remain or EU Settled Status), or a person with limited leave to remain in certain categories.
- The couple is legally married, in a civil partnership, or (under the Unmarried Partner route) has cohabited in a relationship akin to marriage or civil partnership for at least two continuous years.

The applicant must be 18 or older. Applications involving sponsors who are themselves on a temporary visa — for example, a Skilled Worker — are permitted in many cases, but the sponsor's leave to remain must extend beyond the duration of leave sought for the dependant.

### Settled vs. Non-Settled Sponsors: A Comparison

| Sponsor Immigration Status | Can Sponsor Spouse? | Key Constraints |
|---|---|---|
| British Citizen | Yes | Standard Appendix FM requirements apply |
| Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR) | Yes | Standard requirements apply |
| EU Settled Status (EUSS) | Yes | Standard requirements apply |
| EU Pre-Settled Status (EUSS) | Limited | Family permit under EUSS rules; different process |
| Skilled Worker Visa | Yes | Sponsor's leave must cover dependant's intended stay |
| Student Visa | Generally No | Students on most courses cannot sponsor adult dependants |
| Visitor Visa | No | Visitors cannot be sponsors |

## The Financial Requirement: The Most Common Stumbling Block

### The Basic Threshold

As of 2026, the sponsor must show gross annual income of at least **£29,000**. This must come from specified sources, documented in specified ways. The rules do not allow the caseworker discretion: if your evidence does not match the format required by Appendix FM-SE (the supporting evidence appendix), it will be treated as if it does not exist.

**Acceptable income sources include:**

- Salaried employment (most common)
- Non-salaried/variable-hours employment (with specific averaging rules)
- Self-employment income
- Income from property rental
- Pensions
- Certain state benefits (a limited list)
- Cash savings (subject to the formula below)

### The Savings Top-Up Formula

If employment income is below £29,000, savings can bridge the gap. The formula is:

> **Required savings = (Shortfall × 2.5) + £16,000**

**Illustrative Example A:** Priya is a British citizen sponsoring her husband from India. Her gross annual salary is £24,000, leaving a £5,000 shortfall against the £29,000 threshold.

- Shortfall: £5,000
- Required savings: (£5,000 × 2.5) + £16,000 = £12,500 + £16,000 = **£28,500**
- Those savings must have been held in her name (or jointly) in a regulated account for at least six months continuously before the date of application.

**Illustrative Example B:** Marco, an Italian national with EU Settled Status, earns £32,000 per year as a nurse and wants to sponsor his wife from Brazil. His income alone clears the £29,000 threshold. He does not need to demonstrate savings, but he should still provide the six months of payslips and corresponding bank statements required by Appendix FM-SE to evidence the salary.

*These figures are illustrative. Actual outcomes depend on individual circumstances and the current version of the Immigration Rules.*

### What Does NOT Count (Common Traps)

- **Informal cash income** without payslips or a tax record.
- **Income earned abroad** by the applicant when applying from outside the UK (in most entry clearance cases).
- **Gifts or loans**, even if substantial.
- **Savings below the six-month holding period** — timing matters.
- **Cryptocurrency holdings** — not recognised as savings under the rules.

## Accommodation Requirement

The sponsor must show that the couple (and any dependent children) will have adequate accommodation available without recourse to public funds. "Adequate" means the property is not overcrowded within the meaning of the Housing Act 1985 and does not contravene public health regulations.

**Evidence typically required:**

- If renting: current tenancy agreement, landlord letter confirming the applicant can move in, and evidence of the sponsor's residence at the property.
- If the sponsor owns the property: Land Registry documents or mortgage statement.
- If staying with family: letter from the homeowner giving permission, plus evidence of room count versus occupants.

A common oversight is failing to check whether adding the applicant to the household would technically create overcrowding under the Housing Act's occupancy standard. If in doubt, a solicitor or housing adviser can run the calculation.

## Relationship Evidence: What Caseworkers Actually Look For

UKVI assesses whether the relationship is "genuine and subsisting." This is not a box-ticking exercise — caseworkers are looking for a coherent narrative supported by corroborating documents.

**Strong evidence categories:**

- Official documents showing the same address (for couples who have cohabited)
- Photographs across time and location, including third parties who can be identified
- Communication records (call logs, messaging app screenshots with timestamps)
- Evidence of travel to visit each other, including visa stamps and boarding passes
- Joint financial activity: shared accounts, joint insurance, named on each other's correspondence
- Evidence that the sponsor's family and friends know the partner
- Sponsor's supporting statement — written in their own words, specific and personal

**Illustrative Example C:** Lena, a US citizen, met her UK-based husband David online in 2022. They met in person for the first time in late 2022, became engaged in 2023, and married in the US in 2024. Because they lived in different countries throughout their relationship, they cannot provide joint utility bills or a shared tenancy. Instead, their solicitor helped them compile: call logs showing thousands of hours of video calls; flight bookings and boarding passes for five visits; photographs from the visits including with David's parents; David's personal statement describing their relationship timeline; and wedding photographs with named witnesses. The application was approved. This type of long-distance evidence package requires careful structuring, and this is one scenario where a solicitor's experience genuinely adds value.

## The English Language Requirement

| Stage | Required Level | Acceptable Routes |
|---|---|---|
| Entry Clearance (initial application) | A1 CEFR | Approved SELT test; degree taught in English; national of majority English-speaking country |
| First Extension (FLR(M)) | A2 CEFR | As above |
| ILR Application | B1 CEFR | Approved SELT test or degree; Life in the UK Test also required |

Tests must be from UKVI's approved SELT provider list and must be taken at an approved centre. Tests taken with unapproved providers are invalid and will cause a refusal. The list of approved providers is published on GOV.UK and should be checked immediately before booking.

Nationals of the following countries (a non-exhaustive illustrative list) are typically exempt as majority English-speaking country nationals: USA, Canada (for most applicants), Australia, New Zealand, Jamaica, and others — confirm your country's status on GOV.UK.

## 7 Common Mistakes — and How to Avoid Them

1. **Submitting payslips without corresponding bank statements.**
   *Solution:* Appendix FM-SE requires both. Every payslip must be matched to a bank statement showing the salary payment. Prepare these as a paired set.

2. **Using savings that have been in the account for less than six months.**
   *Solution:* Plan ahead. If you intend to use savings to meet the financial requirement, do not move or consolidate funds less than six months before you anticipate applying.

3. **Providing a tenancy agreement that expires before the visa would be granted.**
   *Solution:* Either renew the tenancy before applying or provide evidence that renewal is in progress. An expired or soon-to-expire tenancy raises doubts about adequate accommodation.

4. **Submitting photographs without dates or context.**
   *Solution:* Organise photographs chronologically, include captions or a short index explaining who appears and where the photo was taken. Digital photos with embedded metadata are helpful.

5. **Failing to disclose previous visa refusals.**
   *Solution:* Declare every previous refusal accurately. UKVI cross-references immigration histories. Undisclosed refusals are treated as deception and will result in refusal — and can trigger a ban.

6. **Using a SELT test result that has expired.**
   *Solution:* Check the validity period of your test certificate. Results from most approved providers are valid for two years. Rebook if your result is close to expiry.

7. **Submitting a sponsor statement that is generic or template-like.**
   *Solution:* Write the statement in your own words. Describe how you met, specific moments in the relationship, and why you want to live in the UK together. Caseworkers read thousands of applications; a personal, specific statement stands out.

## When to Use a Solicitor: An Honest Assessment

A solicitor is not legally required. Many couples apply successfully without professional help. But the question is not "can I do this myself?" — it is "what is the cost of a refusal compared to the cost of professional help?"

A UK immigration solicitor regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA), or an OISC-registered immigration adviser at Level 3, can: audit your documents before submission, identify gaps the applicant would not know to look for, draft a covering letter framing your evidence strategically, and advise on borderline financial scenarios.

**Indicative solicitor costs (illustrative, 2026 market):**

- Document review and advice only: £500–£900
- Full preparation and submission support: £1,200–£3,000
- Representation on a refusal appeal: £3,000–£8,000+

Compare this to the cost of a refusal: the application fee (~£1,846) is not refunded. You may also pay for an Immigration Health Surcharge upfront — currently several thousand pounds for a 33-month visa. A refusal means losing these fees and reapplying.

### Scenarios Where a Solicitor Is Strongly Recommended

- Any previous visa refusal for either partner
- Criminal record — even spent convictions in some cases
- Complex or non-standard income (self-employment, multiple jobs, irregular hours)
- Significant gaps in cohabitation evidence for unmarried partners
- The sponsor's own immigration status is on a time-limited visa
- The relationship timeline is complex or the couple have lived apart for extended periods
- Applications involving children from previous relationships

### Scenarios Where DIY is More Realistic

- Both partners have clean immigration histories
- Sponsor is in straightforward salaried employment clearly above the threshold
- Couple has lived together and has ample joint documentation
- The applicant is a national of a majority English-speaking country (no language test required)
- Both partners have time to research and prepare carefully

## The Application Process: Step by Step

1. **Check eligibility** — confirm sponsor status, relationship category, and whether any exemptions or exceptions apply.
2. **Meet the financial requirement** — gather six months of payslips and bank statements; confirm savings if needed.
3. **Prepare relationship evidence** — organise into a chronological bundle with a cover index.
4. **Book and complete the English language test** — allow four to six weeks for results.
5. **Complete the online application form** — via the UKVI Visa4UK or related portal. Accuracy is critical; errors are extremely difficult to correct after submission.
6. **Pay the application fee and Immigration Health Surcharge** — both are payable at the point of submission; neither is refunded on refusal.
7. **Book a biometric appointment** at your local Visa Application Centre (VAC).
8. **Attend the VAC** — submit biometric data and original documents (or copies, per VAC instructions).
9. **Await decision** — standard processing is approximately eight weeks; priority services are available in many countries at additional cost.

## After Arrival: The Route to Settlement

A successful entry clearance grants **33 months** of leave to remain. Before it expires, the spouse must apply for a **further leave to remain (FLR(M))**, which grants an additional 30 months. After completing **five years** on the route, the applicant can apply for **Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR)**, provided they:

- Have not breached the conditions of their leave
- Pass the Life in the UK Test
- Demonstrate B1 English (unless exempt)
- Meet the financial requirement (still applicable at extension and ILR stages)
- Have not spent more than 180 days outside the UK in any 12-month period during the qualifying period

After ILR, British citizenship by naturalisation becomes available after a further qualifying period, subject to residency and good character requirements.
