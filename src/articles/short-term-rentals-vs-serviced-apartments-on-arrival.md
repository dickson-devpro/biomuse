**When you land in a new country, you need a roof over your head before you can do almost anything else — open a bank account, collect a payslip, or sign a permanent lease. Short-term rentals and serviced apartments are the two most practical bridges between your flight and your first real home, but they serve very different needs and carry very different costs.** Understanding the distinction before you book could save a newly arrived foreign national anywhere from several hundred to several thousand dollars during the critical first two months.

## Why This Matters in 2026

The temporary housing market has shifted meaningfully since the post-pandemic normalization of remote and hybrid work. In 2026, two forces are pulling in opposite directions for new arrivals. On one hand, corporate relocation budgets have tightened at many mid-sized employers, pushing more foreign nationals to self-fund their first month of housing rather than rely on a company allowance. On the other hand, the inventory of professionally managed serviced and extended-stay apartment units has expanded substantially in most major US metros, creating genuine competition and, in some markets, downward pressure on nightly rates.

Simultaneously, short-term rental platforms have faced a wave of municipal regulation. Cities from New York to Dallas have introduced registration requirements, minimum-stay rules, and occupancy caps that have removed hundreds of thousands of listings or made them unreliable. For a foreign national with a fixed arrival date tied to a visa start date or an employer's onboarding schedule, a listing disappearing two days before arrival is not a minor inconvenience — it can cascade into missed appointments, delayed bank account openings, and genuine distress.

Meanwhile, the number of international arrivals to the US on employment-based, investor, and family-reunification pathways continues to run at historically significant levels. Whether you are relocating on an L-1 or O-1 visa, arriving as part of a company transfer, or making an early housing decision ahead of pursuing longer-term options described in our guide to [How Immigrants Can Buy a House in the US (2026 Guide)](/how-immigrants-can-buy-a-house-in-the-us/), the temporary housing decision deserves the same analytical rigour you would apply to any significant financial commitment.

## Defining the Two Options Clearly

### Short-Term Rentals (STRs)

Short-term rentals are privately owned residential units — apartments, condos, townhouses, or single-family homes — listed on peer-to-peer platforms such as Airbnb, VRBO, or Furnished Finder. The host is typically an individual landlord or small investor. Pricing is set on a nightly basis, though many hosts offer weekly or monthly discount rates. Services are minimal: you generally get the keys and that is about it. Housekeeping, if it exists at all, is either absent or an add-on cost. Utilities, Wi-Fi, and sometimes even toilet paper are only included if the listing explicitly says so.

The great virtue of STRs is flexibility and variety. You can rent a studio in a neighbourhood you are considering for permanent residence, road-test a commute, and book with a credit card from your home country — no US credit history required. The great risk is platform dependency: the host can cancel, the listing can be removed by the platform for regulatory non-compliance, and the nightly rate structure means costs can escalate sharply if your job start date is delayed or your permanent lease search takes longer than expected.

### Serviced Apartments

Serviced apartments (also called extended-stay hotels, corporate apartments, or apart-hotels in some markets) are professionally managed, fully furnished units operated by a management company. Major operators include brands like Oakwood, Blueground, Sonder's corporate tier, and dozens of regional providers. They are priced monthly (though weekly rates are usually available) and typically bundle utilities, Wi-Fi, basic housekeeping, and building services into a single invoice.

The invoice structure matters more than it might appear. A single, itemised receipt confirming your address, dates of stay, and total payments made is useful when opening business bank accounts, registering with state agencies, or providing proof of address to an employer's HR department. Individual Airbnb hosts rarely provide this kind of documentation without being specifically asked, and even then the format may not satisfy institutional requirements.

## The Real Cost Breakdown: Illustrative Examples

The following examples are **illustrative** and use rounded figures to demonstrate the cost structure. They do not represent current market listings or guarantee any specific pricing.

### Example A: 30-Day Stay, Mid-Sized US City (Illustrative)

**Scenario:** A project manager from Brazil arrives in Charlotte, NC on an L-1 visa. Her employer covers the first 30 days of housing, with a flat $3,500 allowance.

**Short-Term Rental path:**
- Nightly rate on platform: $95/night
- Subtotal (30 nights): $2,850
- Cleaning fee (one-time): $175
- Platform service fee (approx. 14%): $399
- **Total: $3,424**

The total just barely fits the allowance. However, if her start date slips by four days due to visa processing, she needs to either extend the booking (if the host has availability) or find alternative accommodation at the last minute. At $95/night for four additional nights plus another cleaning fee, she could be looking at $555 more — taking her $476 over budget personally.

**Serviced apartment path:**
- Monthly rate (all-in, utilities and Wi-Fi included): $2,900
- Security deposit (waived for corporate booking): $0
- One-time administrative fee: $150
- **Total: $3,050**

She saves $374 versus the STR, has a formal invoice for HR records, can extend month-to-month with 15 days notice, and is not exposed to the same extension-rate risk.

### Example B: 14-Day Stay, High-Cost Market (Illustrative)

**Scenario:** A software engineer from Germany arrives in San Francisco for a 90-day project assignment. He needs two weeks of accommodation before his corporate housing kicks in.

**Short-Term Rental path:**
- Nightly rate: $185/night
- Subtotal (14 nights): $2,590
- Cleaning fee: $225
- Service fee: $362
- **Total: $3,177**

**Serviced apartment path (weekly rate):**
- Weekly rate: $1,450/week
- Two weeks: $2,900
- **Total: $2,900**

At just 14 days, the serviced apartment is still cheaper once platform fees are factored in, though the gap is smaller. The STR wins on variety — he can choose a neighbourhood he actually wants to explore — while the serviced apartment wins on cost and documentation.

**Key takeaway from both examples:** Platform service fees and cleaning fees on STRs are frequently underestimated and can erode apparent nightly-rate savings. Always calculate total out-of-pocket cost, not the headline nightly rate.

## The Credit History Non-Issue (and Where It Still Comes Up)

One of the most common concerns among newly arrived foreign nationals is whether a lack of US credit history will block access to temporary housing. For both STRs and serviced apartments, the answer is generally no: a valid international credit card, passport, and in some cases a letter from an employer are sufficient.

Where the credit question resurfaces is the transition from temporary to permanent housing. Once you are ready to sign a 12-month lease with a private landlord, the absence of a US credit score becomes a genuine obstacle — landlords may require additional deposits, co-signers, or alternative documentation. ## Common Mistakes New Arrivals Make — and How to Avoid Them

1. **Booking an STR without checking local short-term rental regulations**
   *Mistake:* Booking a 30-day Airbnb in New York City without realising the listing violates Local Law 18, which requires hosts to be present and limits entire-unit rentals to fewer than 30 consecutive days. The listing disappears after booking, or is cancelled by the platform.
   *Solution:* Before booking, search the city's official STR registration database, check whether the listing shows a valid registration number, and read recent reviews for any mention of cancellations or regulatory issues. For cities with known regulatory complexity — New York, San Francisco, Los Angeles, Seattle, Miami — default to a serviced apartment operator with a commercial licence.

2. **Choosing accommodation based on headline nightly rate instead of all-in total cost**
   *Mistake:* Selecting an STR at $80/night over a serviced apartment at $2,600/month, failing to add the cleaning fee ($200), service fee ($336), and the cost of utilities not included ($150 estimated).
   *Solution:* Build a total-cost spreadsheet before comparing. Add all mandatory fees — platform service fee, cleaning fee, estimated utility costs if not bundled — and divide by days to get a true daily rate. Only then compare to the serviced apartment's all-in monthly figure.

3. **Not requesting formal address documentation from an STR host**
   *Mistake:* Using an Airbnb address on an employer I-9, bank account application, or state registration without having a letter confirming your stay. The host cannot or will not provide one, causing delays.
   *Solution:* Before booking an STR for any administrative purpose, email the host explicitly and ask whether they can provide a signed letter on their letterhead (or personal letterhead) confirming your name, the property address, and your check-in and check-out dates. If they cannot, book a serviced apartment instead.

4. **Underestimating how long the permanent housing search will take**
   *Mistake:* Booking 14 days of temporary housing, confident a permanent lease will be signed quickly, only to discover that landlords require a US credit score, local pay stubs, or references that a new arrival simply does not yet have. Day 15 arrives with no signed lease and no accommodation booked.
   *Solution:* Budget for at least 30–45 days of temporary housing, not 14, unless you have a guaranteed corporate housing arrangement already confirmed in writing. Extension rates on serviced apartments are predictable; emergency last-minute STR bookings in tight markets are not.

5. **Ignoring the tax and reimbursement documentation trail**
   *Mistake:* Paying for temporary housing out of pocket on the assumption the employer will reimburse, then discovering the employer requires itemised VAT or sales-tax-compliant receipts that an individual Airbnb host cannot provide.
   *Solution:* Confirm your employer's reimbursement receipt requirements before booking. If a formal invoice is required, book directly with a serviced apartment operator who can generate one. Keep all booking confirmations, credit card statements, and any correspondence with hosts.

6. **Assuming pet policies are consistent across platforms**
   *Mistake:* Booking a pet-friendly STR listing only to arrive and find the host has a separate, un-listed agreement that bans dogs over 25 lbs, affecting a large-breed dog.
   *Solution:* Communicate explicitly with the host via the platform's messaging system (creating a written record) about your specific animal before booking. Ask for written confirmation that your pet is permitted. For serviced apartments, request the pet policy in the lease or booking confirmation email.

## Timing Your Move from Temporary to Permanent Housing

The optimal exit from temporary housing is a signed standard lease with a private landlord, which almost always offers a lower effective monthly rate than either an STR or a serviced apartment. The challenge is that this transition requires documents you may not yet have on arrival: US credit history, local pay stubs, bank account statements, and in some cases a Social Security Number (SSN) or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN).

Foreign nationals should begin gathering the supporting documentation for a permanent lease application during their temporary housing phase, not after. This means:

- Opening a US bank account within the first week (your serviced apartment address and passport are usually sufficient for most challenger banks and credit unions)
- Requesting an employer letter confirming your position, compensation, and start date
- Beginning the process of establishing a US credit history through a secured credit card or credit-builder product as early as week two
- Understanding what ITIN-based lease applications look like, since not all landlords require an SSN — our [First-Time Renters Guide for New Arrivals in the US 2026](/first-time-renters-guide-new-arrivals-us/) covers landlord documentation requirements in full

The typical window from arrival to signed permanent lease for a foreign national without an established US financial profile is four to eight weeks. Plan your temporary housing budget accordingly.

## Making the Final Decision: A Framework

Use this decision framework to choose between an STR and a serviced apartment on arrival:

**Choose a serviced apartment if:**
- Your stay is 21 days or longer
- You need formal address documentation for employment, banking, or administrative purposes
- You are in a city with known short-term rental regulatory volatility
- Your arrival date is tied to a hard deadline (visa activation date, employment start date)
- Your employer will reimburse costs and requires formal invoices
- Extension risk is not something you can afford

**Choose a short-term rental if:**
- Your stay is 7–14 days or fewer
- You want to explore multiple neighbourhoods before committing to an area for permanent housing
- Your city has stable STR regulations and you have verified listing compliance
- Variety, local character, or specific location are priorities over cost certainty
- A trusted local contact has personally recommended a specific host

Neither option is universally superior. The right answer depends on the length of your stay, your documentation needs, the regulatory environment of your destination city, and your personal risk tolerance. What is clear is that neither option should be chosen based on headline price alone — total cost, reliability, and documentation must all factor in.
